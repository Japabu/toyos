# The binary-file ledger's scope question dies with the directory that raised it

`NOTICE` names every committed third-party file with its hash, its upstream and
its licence. It counted nothing in the deleted document tree, and this entry
asked whether it should have: that tree carried 12,606,601 bytes of the owner's
own photographs of his own panel, committed because they were the only record of
the `rip` that named #156 and a scratchpad does not survive a session. They
shipped in nothing.

**Nothing is owed once that tree is gone.** The photographs go with it, and the
committed binaries that remain are already disposed of: the ones under `assets/`
and `ovmf/` each have a `NOTICE` entry, and the ones that do not — `first-boot.jpg`
and `doom.jpg` at the root, 3,534,095 bytes of the owner's own screenshots — are
his own work, with no upstream, no foreign licence, and nothing for a ledger of
third-party obligations to make findable. No directory of committed evidence
survives to re-raise the question: evidence lives in commit messages and in git
history now.

**So this entry should be deleted in the commit that deletes the tree it is
about.** It is kept only until then, so the question is visibly answered rather
than silently dropped.
